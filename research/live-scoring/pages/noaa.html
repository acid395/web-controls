    National Hydrologic Discussion - EXPERIMENTAL
    NWS National Water Center - Tuscaloosa AL
    700 AM CDT FRI SEP 25 2026

    .Update...

    The main change to this mornings issuance of the Flood Hazard Outlook was
    to expand the New England Limited area southward and rename it to the
    Northeast. Additionally, the timing for Hawaiis Considerable and Limited
    flood impact areas was changed to through day 3".

    Elsewhere, there are no additional changes with this issuance.

    //GKendrick

    Previous discussion as follows:

    ...Considerable to locally catastrophic flash flooding possible in Hawaii...
    Flash flooding impacts remain possible across portions of the Southwest...
    Flash and urban flooding possible in Southern New England... Moderate to
    major coastal flooding expected along the Coastal Mid-Atlantic...

    .Hawaii...
    Considerable flooding impacts, some of which may be locally catastrophic,
    remain possible for the southern and eastern portions of the Big Island on
    Friday and into the weekend as Tropical Storm Nolo is forecast to produce
    15 - 20" with a maximum of 35" possible. Outer bands of TS Nolo bringing
    additional rainfall later today in combination with hydrologic sensitivity
    of these areas from previous heavy rainfall supports the increased
    confidence of considerable impacts, including life-threatening flash
    flooding along with mudslides in areas of steep terrain. The remainder of
    the Big Island and Maui County are in a Limited selection due to the
    possibility of locally considerable flooding impacts. TS Nolo is forecast
    to produce totals of 5 - 10" across Maui County with a maximum total of
    about 20" possible. No changes were made to the existing Limited and
    Considerable areas across the Big Island and Maui County for the afternoon
    issuance.

    .Southwest...
    Flash flooding impacts remain possible for portions of the Texas and
    Oklahoma Panhandles, southern and eastern New Mexico and into far west
    Texas as another surge of monsoonal moisture brings repeated thunderstorms
    and heavy rainfall. The greatest rainfall amounts are forecast along and
    east of the Sacramento Mountains, where multiple days of heavy rainfall
    have increased the hydrological sensitivity of these basins. Overall wetter
    antecedent conditions across the majority of the area of concern as a
    result of multiple days of heavy rainfall will help support more efficient
    runoff and stronger hydrologic responses. Rapid runoff remains possible in
    small streams, creeks, dry washes, along with flash flooding and debris
    flows near burned areas and steep terrain. Antecedent conditions are more
    dry in far west Texas and this may initially mitigate runoff, but repeated
    rainfall could eventually overwhelm soils and initiate runoff and quick
    rises on small streams. The GFS- and NBM-forced National Water Model
    signals have shown more isolated responses and lower probabilities for
    rapid onset flooding across the area of concern, which has lowered
    confidence on the exact location of greatest impacts. For the afternoon
    issuance, small adjustments were made to the limited area to account for
    the latest trends in QPF and ongoing watches/warnings across the region.


    .Coastal Mid-Atlantic...
    Moderate to major coastal flooding is expected to continue along portions
    of the Mid-Atlantic coastline from North Carolina to New York into this
    weekend as a result of astronomical high tides and a coastal low generating
    onshore flow to the region.

    .Southern New England...
    Flash and urban flooding is possible beginning tonight and through the
    weekend as a Nor'easter moves into the region. There is model agreement
    that the region will receive rainfall, but there is uncertainty as to the
    specific regions that will see the greatest flooding impacts.

    .Central Plains...
    Flooding impacts are possible this weekend and next week as the region
    receives several rounds of rainfall over the next several days. Antecedent
    conditions are fairly dry, but repeated rounds of rainfall could eventually
    overwhelm infiltration capacity and generate rapid runoff. The cumulative
    rainfall could also increase the river flood threat later in the forecast
    period. Guidance from both the GFS and NBM-forced National Water Model
    currently shows annual exceedance probabilities of 20-50%, suggesting
    agreement among the models on the region seeing potential flooding impacts.

    //Cordak/EAS
